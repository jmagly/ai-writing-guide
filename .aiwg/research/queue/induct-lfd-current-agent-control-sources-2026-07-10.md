### 1. Reward Hacking Benchmark: Measuring Exploits in LLM Agents with Tool Use

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02964  
**Type**: paper  
**Priority**: high  
**Induction status**: Complete in `section9/research-papers` as `REF-1500`
(`documentation/references/REF-1500-thaman-2026-reward-hacking-benchmark.md`;
PDF `pdfs/full/REF-1500-thaman-2026-reward-hacking-benchmark.pdf`; citation
sidecar `documentation/citations/REF-1500-citations.md`; radar sidecar
`documentation/radar/REF-1500-radar.md`).  
**Why relevant**: Provides an agent-specific reward-hacking benchmark with
instrumented exploit detection, hidden/gated evaluation data, and environmental
hardening. This directly supports AIWG's LFD rule tiering, VOID semantics, and
hidden holdout controls.

**Key claims to verify from full paper**:

- Reward-hacking propensity increases with task-chain complexity and task
  difficulty.
- Environmental hardening can reduce exploit rates without degrading honest
  task success.
- Some grader components and hidden evaluation data are intentionally gated to
  preserve benchmark diagnostic utility.

### 2. Inference-Time Budget Control for LLM Search Agents

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05701  
**Type**: paper  
**Priority**: high  
**Induction status**: Complete in `section9/research-papers` as `REF-1501`
(`documentation/references/REF-1501-fang-2026-inference-time-budget-control.md`;
PDF `pdfs/full/REF-1501-fang-2026-inference-time-budget-control.pdf`; citation
sidecar `documentation/citations/REF-1501-citations.md`; radar sidecar
`documentation/radar/REF-1501-radar.md`).  
**Why relevant**: Frames search-agent performance as step-level budget control,
not merely a larger test-time-compute problem. Supports AIWG's requirement for
per-step budget observability, low-yield action abandonment, and budget stop
reports.

**Key claims to verify from full paper**:

- Search agents spend budget through branches, intermediate states, and
  low-yield actions, not only output tokens.
- Budget-aware inference must decide where to spend the next unit of budget.
- Step-level intervention is needed to detect and abandon failing trajectories
  before they silently exhaust resources.

### 3. Budget-Aware Tool-Use Enables Effective Agent Scaling

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17006  
**Type**: paper  
**Priority**: high  
**Induction status**: Complete in `section9/research-papers` as `REF-1502`
(`documentation/references/REF-1502-liu-2025-budget-aware-tool-use-agent-scaling.md`;
PDF `pdfs/full/REF-1502-liu-2025-budget-aware-tool-use-agent-scaling.pdf`;
citation sidecar `documentation/citations/REF-1502-citations.md`; radar sidecar
`documentation/radar/REF-1502-radar.md`).  
**Why relevant**: Gives a concrete agent-scaling pattern with per-tool budgets,
early stopping, verification, and unified token/tool cost metrics. Supports the
AIWG loop budget object and speed-of-accuracy scoring.

**Key claims to verify from full paper**:

- Tool budgets can be first-class stopping conditions.
- Early stopping after successful verification captures accuracy/resource
  tradeoffs better than forcing agents to exhaust budgets.
- Unified cost metrics across tokens and tool calls are necessary for comparing
  agent trajectories.

### 4. Spend Less, Reason Better: Budget-Aware Value Tree Search for LLM Agents

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12634  
**Type**: paper  
**Priority**: medium  
**Induction status**: Complete as `REF-1503` on 2026-07-10. Artifacts:
`documentation/references/REF-1503-li-2026-budget-aware-value-tree-search.md`,
`documentation/citations/REF-1503-citations.md`,
`documentation/radar/REF-1503-radar.md`, and
`pdfs/full/REF-1503-li-2026-budget-aware-value-tree-search.pdf`.  
**GRADE**: B+  
**Why relevant**: Addresses constrained-compute agent performance and argues
that unrestricted test-time compute leads to redundant or low-yield action
spending. Supports AIWG's dosed entropy and diminishing-returns controls.

**Key claims to verify from full paper**:

- Current agents often lack fine-grained budget control.
- Blindly adding resources can produce diminishing returns after excessive
  spending.
- Intermediate step-level control helps avoid dead ends and repeated low-yield
  trajectories.
