# External research corpus audit: reasoning and architecture

## Decision summary

The corpus contains **57 sources with sufficient signal to enter AIWG's product-planning backlog**. They do not imply 57 independent features. They resolve into 12 epics, led by system-level multi-agent evaluation, trace-driven harness optimization, durable checkpoint/recovery, adversarial authorization, and governed autonomous research. Another 8 superficially relevant source decisions are explicitly excluded because AIWG already incorporates them, they duplicate a stronger source, or their evidence is too weak.

The architecture recommendation is conservative about kernel growth. Only cross-cutting invariants belong in the kernel: execution contracts, authorization, trace/evaluation schemas, checkpoint semantics, and routing policy. Experimental optimizers, benchmarks, and domain workflows should remain addons or extensions until they demonstrate provider portability. A new framework is justified only where a distinct lifecycle, artifact graph, roles, gates, and governance model exist.

## Audit method and evidence boundary

- Loaded with `aiwg show`: `aiwg-model-reasoning-worker`, `research-quality-audit`, `grade-on-ingest`, Technical Researcher (`aiwg:agent:d9f602c7e4867abf`), and Architecture Designer (`aiwg:agent:48dbdb489396308d`). The research quick reference was also followed.
- Wrapper metadata declares `model: opus`, `model-role: reasoning`, and `model-tier: premium`. That proves routing intent, not the provider's hidden runtime identity; the worker has no independent runtime-model introspection surface.
- Read-only inventory found **1,901** `documentation/references/REF-*.md` analyses, **1,390 PDFs**, and **8,515 Markdown files** in the corpus.
- Selection used local full analysis documents, their source metadata, reported methods/results, limitations, and GRADE-like ratings. `HIGH`, `MODERATE`, and `LOW` below express confidence in the architectural inference, not a new reassessment of the underlying study.
- AIWG overlap was checked against the current repository and `aiwg discover` results for coordination, recovery, memory, evaluation, security, human authorization, research, preservation, and cost-aware routing.

## Architecture gates before incorporation

1. **Evidence gate:** no P0/P1 feature moves from proposal to implementation without a source assessment, explicit limitations, and at least one independent or peer-reviewed supporting source where the selected item is a preprint/blog.
2. **Kernel gate:** a kernel change must be provider-neutral, cross-framework, enforceable, and cheaper than repeated local implementations. Otherwise it remains an addon/extension.
3. **Evaluation gate:** every new orchestration or memory mechanism needs held-out tasks, cost/latency, failure taxonomy, and baseline comparison. Output quality alone is insufficient.
4. **Security gate:** self-improvement, memory evolution, tool learning, and autonomous research require quarantine, least privilege, provenance, rollback, and independent promotion authority.
5. **Model-routing gate:** compare model tier and harness jointly; a result from one model/harness pairing must not be generalized to “the model.”
6. **Lifecycle gate for frameworks:** `research-lab` and multi-agent governance qualify as framework-scale only after defining roles, artifacts, transitions, gates, archive/provenance, and operator controls.

## Recommended next slice

Start with E1–E5 as an architecture elaboration, not immediate broad implementation. Produce: (a) a provider-neutral agent-run trace schema, (b) consistent mission checkpoint ADR, (c) model×harness evaluation manifest, (d) adversarial authorization threat model, and (e) a held-out suite exercising topology, stateful tools, recovery, and reward-hacking. Those artifacts create the measurement and safety foundation needed before E6–E12 can be incorporated responsibly.
